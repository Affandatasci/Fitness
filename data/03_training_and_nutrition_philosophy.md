# Forge Physique Coaching — Training & Nutrition Philosophy

## Training Philosophy

### Core Principles

Forge Physique Coaching's training approach is built on four principles that apply to every client, regardless of tier or experience level:

1. **Progressive overload above all else.** Long-term muscle and strength gain comes from gradually increasing the demand placed on the body — more weight, more reps, more sets, or better technique over time. Every program is structured around a clear progression scheme, not random daily workouts.
2. **Consistency beats intensity.** A moderately hard session done 4 times a week for a year beats an all-out session done sporadically. Programs are built at a training volume the client can actually sustain long-term, not a volume that looks impressive for two weeks and then causes burnout or injury.
3. **Compound movements first.** Every program is built around a foundation of compound lifts (squat, deadlift, bench press, overhead press, rows, pull-ups, or their appropriate variations/regressions) before accessory work is added.
4. **Individualization within a proven framework.** The underlying training principles (progressive overload, adequate volume, sufficient recovery) are the same for every client, but the specific exercises, split, and weekly structure are built around the client's equipment access, schedule, injury history, and experience level.

### Training Split Structure

Training splits are assigned based on the client's weekly availability, not a one-size-fits-all template:

- **3 days/week available:** Full-body split
- **4 days/week available:** Upper/Lower split
- **5–6 days/week available:** Push/Pull/Legs (PPL) split, repeated across the week

Program updates happen every 4 weeks, incorporating the client's logged performance data (weights, reps, RPE — Rate of Perceived Exertion) from the prior 4 weeks.

### Equipment Adaptability

Forge Physique Coaching designs programs around whatever equipment the client actually has access to:

- **Full commercial gym:** Full barbell, dumbbell, and machine programming
- **Home gym (barbell + rack + dumbbells):** Adapted programming using available equipment, prioritizing the same compound movement patterns
- **Minimal equipment (dumbbells only, or bodyweight):** Programs are still built around progressive overload principles using the tools available — this is a valid long-term training approach, not a temporary compromise

### Injury & Limitation Accommodation

During onboarding, every client completes a movement history and injury questionnaire. Programs are adjusted around any reported limitations (e.g., a client with a prior shoulder injury is programmed around overhead pressing variations that don't aggravate it). Forge Physique Coaching is not a physical therapy service — clients with an active, unresolved injury are asked to get clearance from a physician or physical therapist before starting, and Forge Physique Coaching will coordinate programming around any restrictions that professional provides.

### Deload & Recovery

Every training block includes a planned deload (reduced volume/intensity week) approximately every 6–8 weeks, or sooner if a client's logged RPE data suggests accumulating fatigue. Rest is treated as a programmed part of the plan, not an afterthought.

## Nutrition Philosophy

### Core Approach

Forge Physique Coaching's nutrition guidance is built on flexible dieting principles: hitting a personalized calorie and macronutrient (protein, carbohydrate, fat) target while allowing the client to choose which foods fit those numbers, rather than prescribing a rigid list of "allowed" and "banned" foods.

### Why Flexible Dieting

Rigid, restrictive diets have a well-documented pattern of high short-term compliance and high long-term failure — clients white-knuckle through a restrictive plan, then abandon it entirely. Flexible dieting is chosen specifically because it is sustainable as a long-term approach, not just a program-length approach.

### How Targets Are Set

Every client's calorie and macro targets are calculated individually based on:
- Current body weight and estimated body composition
- Activity level (both training and general daily activity, NEAT)
- Stated goal (fat loss, muscle gain, or recomposition)
- Training experience and response history (adjusted over time based on actual results, not just formulas)

Targets are reassessed every 4 weeks based on the client's logged weight trend and adherence, and adjusted up or down as needed.

### Protein Priority

Regardless of the specific goal, protein intake is prioritized above all other nutrition variables, generally in the range of 0.7–1g per pound of bodyweight, adjusted per client. Protein is treated as the non-negotiable target; carbohydrate and fat allocation is more flexible around the client's preferences.

### Tracking Approach

New clients are asked to track intake (via an app such as MyFitnessPal or Cronometer) for at least the first 8–12 weeks, to build accurate awareness of portion sizes and food composition. Clients who reach their goals and demonstrate consistent adherence may transition to a less granular "portion and habit-based" approach for maintenance, at Marcus's discretion, on a case-by-case basis.

### Meal Timing

Forge Physique Coaching does not prescribe rigid meal timing (e.g., "eat every 3 hours") for most clients — total daily intake matters far more than timing for the vast majority of goals. Peri-workout nutrition timing (protein/carbs around training) is discussed individually for clients with performance-specific goals.

## Supplement Stance

Forge Physique Coaching takes a minimalist, evidence-first stance on supplements. The only supplements routinely recommended are:

- **Creatine monohydrate** (3–5g daily) — strong, consistent research support for strength and muscle gain
- **A basic whey or plant protein powder** — for convenience in hitting protein targets, not a requirement
- **Vitamin D** — only if blood work or geographic/lifestyle factors suggest low levels

Forge Physique Coaching does not sell, promote, or receive commission on any supplement brand. Fat burners, "detox" products, and proprietary blend supplements are actively discouraged due to weak or absent research support and, in some cases, safety concerns.

## Alcohol & Social Life

Forge Physique Coaching does not require complete abstinence from alcohol or social eating. Guidance is given on how to fit occasional social drinking or eating out into the client's overall calorie target, because a program that cannot coexist with a normal social life is not a program most clients can sustain.

## Cardio Philosophy

Cardio is programmed as a tool in service of the client's specific goal, not a mandatory daily requirement:

- **Fat loss phases:** Moderate cardio (2–4 sessions/week) is typically added to support the calorie deficit and cardiovascular health
- **Muscle gain phases:** Minimal cardio (1–2 sessions/week) is maintained mainly for general health, not for calorie burn
- **Method:** Walking (often tracked via daily step target) is prioritized as the primary cardio tool for most clients due to its low fatigue cost, with structured cardio machine sessions added as needed

## Sample Weekly Split — Upper/Lower (4 Days/Week)

To make the training philosophy concrete, here is a representative Upper/Lower split structure used for a client training 4 days per week:

| Day | Focus | Example Movements |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Upper (Push emphasis) | Bench press, overhead press, incline dumbbell press, triceps accessory |
| Tuesday | Lower (Quad emphasis) | Back squat, leg press, walking lunge, calf raise |
| Wednesday | Rest / light activity | Walking, mobility work |
| Thursday | Upper (Pull emphasis) | Barbell row, lat pulldown, face pull, biceps accessory |
| Friday | Lower (Hip/hamstring emphasis) | Deadlift or Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, leg curl, core work |
| Saturday–Sunday | Rest or optional light cardio | Walking, recreational activity |

Exact exercise selection, sets, reps, and load are individualized per client based on intake data and updated every 4 weeks.

## Rep Range Guidance by Goal

Forge Physique Coaching uses rep ranges as a tool matched to the client's current phase and goal, not a single fixed number applied everywhere:

- **Strength-focused phases:** Primarily 3–6 reps on main compound lifts, at higher relative intensity
- **Hypertrophy (muscle growth) phases:** Primarily 6–12 reps across most exercises, which the current research base supports as the most efficient range for muscle growth when taken close to effort
- **Fat loss phases:** Rep ranges are maintained similarly to whatever phase preceded the cut (strength or hypertrophy ranges) — the goal during a fat loss phase is to preserve muscle and strength, not to switch to "toning" style high-rep training, which is not supported by current research as superior for fat loss or muscle definition

## Understanding RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)

Clients log RPE — a 1–10 scale of how hard a set felt — alongside weight and reps for every logged set. This is used instead of (or alongside) percentage-based programming because it accounts for daily fluctuations in energy, sleep, and stress that a fixed percentage cannot:

- **RPE 10:** Absolute maximum effort, could not have done another rep
- **RPE 8–9:** 1–2 reps left in the tank
- **RPE 7 or below:** Comfortable, several reps in reserve

Most working sets in the program are targeted in the RPE 7–9 range, which research supports as effective for both strength and hypertrophy while managing fatigue and injury risk better than constant RPE 10 training.

## Micronutrients, Fiber & Hydration

Beyond calories and macros, Forge Physique Coaching guidance includes:

- **Fiber target:** Generally 25–35g/day, supported primarily through vegetables, fruit, and whole grains, both for digestive health and satiety during fat loss phases
- **Micronutrients:** Rather than prescribing individual vitamin targets, clients are guided toward a varied whole-food diet that naturally covers most micronutrient needs; specific deficiencies (e.g., low vitamin D) are addressed only if flagged by the client's own blood work
- **Hydration:** A general guideline of roughly half the client's bodyweight (in pounds) in ounces of water per day, adjusted upward for heat, activity level, and individual sweat rate

## Sleep & Recovery

Sleep is treated as a core recovery variable, not an afterthought. Clients log estimated sleep hours as part of their check-in data, and consistent under-7-hours sleep is flagged and discussed as a limiting factor on both training performance and nutrition adherence — often before any program adjustment is made, since insufficient sleep can undermine even a well-designed program.

## Cardio Protocol Specifics

- **LISS (Low-Intensity Steady State):** Extended walking or easy cycling, used as the default cardio tool due to low recovery cost — typically tracked via a daily step target (commonly 8,000–12,000 steps depending on the client's fat loss phase and starting activity level)
- **MISS (Moderate-Intensity Steady State):** Incline treadmill walking, cycling, or rowing at a sustained moderate effort, added when additional calorie expenditure is needed beyond what step count alone provides
- **HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training):** Used sparingly and only for clients who specifically enjoy it or have a performance goal that benefits from it — not used as a default fat-loss tool, since its recovery cost can interfere with strength training quality

## Common Client Mistakes Addressed Early

Based on patterns across hundreds of coached clients, Forge Physique Coaching proactively addresses these common mistakes during onboarding rather than waiting for them to become a problem:

1. **Chasing the scale daily and reacting emotionally to normal weight fluctuation** — addressed by teaching clients to look at weekly trend averages, not single daily readings
2. **Under-eating protein while over-restricting overall calories** — addressed by making protein the first and most non-negotiable target set during intake
3. **Programming too much cardio too early in a fat loss phase**, leaving no room to increase cardio later when progress stalls — addressed by starting cardio conservatively and adding it progressively
4. **Abandoning the program after a single bad week** — addressed directly through the check-in relationship, where a bad week is discussed and contextualized rather than left to spiral into quitting
